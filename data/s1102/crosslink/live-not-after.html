How the no-tax-on-overtime deduction works

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act added a temporary above-the-line deduction for qualified overtime compensation, for tax years 2025 through 2028. A chunk of your overtime pay gets subtracted from your income before income tax is figured, so you keep more of it. Payroll taxes (Social Security and Medicare) still apply.

The deduction is capped at $12,500 if you file single, $25,000 on a joint return. It phases out above $150,000 / $300,000. And it only applies to the premium portion of your overtime, the extra half-time pay on hours over 40, not your straight-time wages. If your income is too high, this calculator tells you $0 rather than inventing a number.

Is overtime taxed at a higher rate?

No, that’s a myth. Overtime is taxed at your ordinary marginal rate, the same as regular wages. The confusion comes from a bigger paycheck pushing your annual total into a higher bracket. But only the dollars above each threshold get taxed at the higher rate.

Common questions

Who qualifies for no tax on overtime?

Generally, non-exempt (typically hourly) employees who earn FLSA overtime. The deduction applies to the overtime premium reported as overtime on your W-2. Salaried exempt employees generally do not qualify.

Does this eliminate all tax on my overtime?

No. The deduction eliminates federal income tax on the qualifying portion, up to the cap. FICA (Social Security + Medicare) still applies. And the deduction phases out at higher incomes.

How do I claim the no-tax-on-overtime deduction?

Claim it when you file your 2025–2028 return. To see the savings in your paycheck sooner, update your Form W-4 with your employer using the IRS withholding guidance.

Does my state honor this deduction?

It depends on your state’s conformity with federal tax law. States with no income tax benefit automatically. Conforming states likely pass it through. Some states may decouple.
